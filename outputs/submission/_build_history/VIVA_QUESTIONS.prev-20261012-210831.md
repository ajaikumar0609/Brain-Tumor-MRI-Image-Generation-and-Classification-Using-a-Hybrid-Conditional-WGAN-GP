# Viva questions and answers (final, de-duplicated experiment)

All numbers come from `outputs/deduplicated_experiment/` (verified: 23/23 checks). Questions 1-20 come from the verified question set; 21-23 were added for the submission package.

1. **Q: What was the problem with the original train/test split?** A: About two thirds of the original Testing images had near-identical, often mirrored, copies in Training, and 56% of CNN-validation images had copies in CNN-training; exact hashing could not see them. So earlier test numbers were optimistic.
2. **Q: How was the new split built?** A: All 7,200 images were pooled; near-duplicates were found with 32×32 cosine similarity over 8 orientations (threshold 0.98), grouped by connected components into 3,479 clusters, refined at 96×96, label-conflict and augmented-file clusters were quarantined, one representative per cluster was kept, and clusters were split 70/15/15 per class.
3. **Q: How many images remain?** A: 3,295 unique images: train 2,311, validation 492, test 492 (no-tumour: 227/46/46).
4. **Q: Is the new split guaranteed leak-free?** A: No. It prevents copies and mirrored duplicates detected by the method from crossing partitions; same-patient neighbouring slices cannot be detected without patient IDs, and rescaled or heavily re-cropped copies below the cut may remain.
5. **Q: Why was the old Testing folder not reused as test?** A: Because it overlaps Training; after pooling and re-partitioning by cluster, the new test partition is the only valid final-evaluation set.
6. **Q: Which GAN was best?** A: DCGAN: FID 154.7, KID x1000 129.4; cWGAN-GP 177.8 / 157.6; WGAN-GP 182.1 / 163.5; floor 34.2.
7. **Q: Is the cWGAN-GP better than WGAN-GP?** A: Slightly on both metrics (4.3 FID), but with one training run each and a few seed standard deviations of margin, so it is weak evidence. The runs are also not compute-matched against DCGAN.
8. **Q: Why can't these FID values be compared with the earlier ones?** A: The reference set changed (n=492 instead of 1,600) and FID is biased at small n; even the real-vs-real floor rose from 14 to 34.
9. **Q: Describe the downstream conditions.** A: A: real training images (2,311). B: A plus 2,888 cWGAN-GP images (requested labels). C: A resampled with replacement to B's size (5,199) and class mix, with no synthetic images.
10. **Q: What were the downstream results?** A: Test accuracy A 0.817 ± 0.027, B 0.839 ± 0.020, C 0.869 ± 0.021; macro-F1 0.786, 0.821, 0.853 (3 seeds, n=492).
11. **Q: Does synthetic augmentation help?** A: B beat A in 3/3 seeds, but C (no synthetic data) was higher than B on average and in 2/3 seeds. So there is no evidence the synthetic images add benefit beyond training volume; with 3 seeds and n=492 the difference between B and C is not statistically resolved.
12. **Q: Why was condition C needed and what does it not control?** A: B has more examples and optimiser steps than A. C matches size and class mix without synthetic data. It does not control for diversity: repeated real images are exact copies.
13. **Q: How uncertain are the accuracy numbers?** A: A single run on 492 test images has a 95% interval of about ±3.2 points; per-class results, especially no-tumour (n=46), are much less certain.
14. **Q: What does the class-confusion KID show?** A: Row-wise only meningioma is closest to its own class; column-wise the generated label is closest for glioma and notumor. Labels change the output (no-tumour images are brighter) but this is not evidence of tumour-specific or medically correct images.
15. **Q: What happened to the cWGAN-GP training?** A: The first attempt stopped at step 2861 with a non-finite critic loss/gradient after healthy losses; no Metal error was logged, the Mac was later found rebooted, and the cause is unconfirmed. It was resumed from the verified step-2,500 checkpoint and completed 3,000 generator / 15,000 critic updates; the failed attempt's logs are preserved.
16. **Q: How do you know each GAN used only the training partition?** A: Each checkpoint records the SHA-256 of its training file list, which equals the hash of the manifest's training representatives; validation, test and quarantined images are excluded; 23 verification checks passed.
17. **Q: Validation vs test metrics?** A: Validation (492 images) only chooses the best epoch; headline numbers are test accuracies of that epoch; the test partition is read only after training.
18. **Q: What are the main limitations?** A: Small imbalanced data, heuristic de-duplication with possible patient-level leakage, 3 seeds, single GAN runs, short training, unverified synthetic labels and dataset source, Inception features on small grayscale MRI.
19. **Q: Can this be used clinically?** A: No. The images are not shown to contain anatomically accurate tumours, labels are unverified and the classifier is a small research model.
20. **Q: What did you find about the dataset itself?** A: Heavy duplication (only 3,295 of 7,200 images are unique), augmented files in both folders, and 8 clusters (30 images) with conflicting class labels, i.e. label noise.
21. **Q: What would you do next?** A: Obtain patient identifiers for patient-level splits, collect more unique data, run more seeds and training runs with significance tests, train longer, verify synthetic labels, and confirm the dataset source and licence.
22. **Q: Where did the dataset come from, and what is its licence?** A: It was downloaded from Kaggle in Chrome, but the exact page and licence could not be established: no URL or Kaggle metadata is recorded, and the data (1,400/400 images per class, files marked `aug`) does not match the public 5,712/1,311 split that the starter README named. The source and licence are unverified, and the dataset is not redistributed.
23. **Q: Why is the final test set so small and what does that mean?** A: After removing near-duplicates only 3,295 of 7,200 images are unique, leaving 492 test images (46 no-tumour). A single accuracy has about ±3 points of sampling error and per-class figures, especially no-tumour, are much less certain.
24. **Q: What hardware did you use and how long did it take?** A: A MacBook Air (M4, 16 GB) using PyTorch's MPS GPU backend. Retraining took about 5 min (DCGAN), 29 min (WGAN-GP) and 36 min (cWGAN-GP, cumulative) while Low Power Mode was on; downstream training and FID/KID each took a few minutes.
